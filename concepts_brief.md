# Pricing Objection Handling eLearning (Rise 360, 30 min)

* **Author:** linuxsunil
* **Date:** 2026-11-09

## Overview

Sales reps at a B2B SaaS company lose deals when a prospect pushes back on price. A typical case is "your price is 40% higher than the competitor." Reps respond by offering a discount right away instead of defending value. Managers then rescue deals or lose them. Reps have battle cards and competitor sheets, so the knowledge exists. Reps do not use it under pressure.

The proposed fix is a 30-minute Rise 360 SCORM course for 50 new reps (2–3 years of selling experience, product training only, no objection-handling training). It covers five pricing objections:

1. "Your price is too high."
2. "Competitor X is cheaper."
3. "We don't have budget right now."
4. "Can you match their price?"
5. "What do we get that justifies the premium?"

**Target behavior:** Within 30 seconds of a pricing objection, the rep gives a value-based response before mentioning price or any discount. The response has three parts: acknowledge the concern, pivot to a differentiator, and ask a qualifying question.

**Constraints:** Rise 360 only (no Storyline). No video. No voiceover. SCORM-compliant. Three-week build. One instructional designer.

**Success measure (as revised by author):** A single-page manager observation checklist, delivered as a job aid with the course, covers the three-part response criteria. Manager observation of two live calls per rep is the observed measure. Discount rate by rep is a 90-day lagging indicator, not a pass/fail gate. Sales ops holds the baseline.

**Scope (as revised by author):** The course builds the cognitive framework: the three-part response structure and five objection scripts. Live rehearsal (a 15-minute role-play with the manager after the course) is a separate required activity outside the eLearning. It will appear in a blended design note in the brief.

## Analysis

This section combines the findings of four Goldfish reviewers: alignment, learner, practicality, and theory. Each reviewer read the hypothesis and returned findings. No reviewer wrote files. Severity labels are the reviewers' own.

### Blocking issues (resolve before the spec proceeds)

1. **No in-course activity or assessment for the target behavior.** (Alignment) The only measure is manager observation of live calls, which sits outside the course. The course itself has no practice or check that the behavior is learned. The chain from objective to activity to assessment is broken at both ends.
2. **The root cause has no objective or measure.** (Alignment) The stated cause is that confidence collapses under emotional pushback. Nothing in the design can show that confidence changed.
3. **Live pressure cannot be reproduced in a text-and-click course.** (Theory, Practicality) Rehearsal under pressure needs spoken, timed, emotionally charged practice. Rise 360 with no voice and no video cannot provide that. The course can teach the response structure and practice choosing responses. Live rehearsal has to happen somewhere else, such as a manager or peer role-play. Alternatively, the target must be narrowed to what the course can build. The current hypothesis does not choose between these.
4. **No context of use.** (Learner) The hypothesis does not say when reps take the course (before calls, during a training week, under quota pressure), on what device, or how it fits their day. The confidence design depends on this.
5. **No subject-matter expert named.** (Practicality) The five value-first responses need approved value claims, competitive differentiators, and qualifying questions. Without a named SME who signs off, the content cannot be drafted, and the differentiator step has no source.
6. **Manager time for the success measure is unresourced.** (Practicality) The measure needs 100 observed live calls (50 reps × 2). Manager capacity, calendar access, and an observation form are not stated.

### Major issues

- **Experienced reps may reject a novice script.** (Learner) Reps with 2–3 years of experience have a discount habit and their own pitch. The course has to acknowledge that habit and explain why value-first works better for them.
- **Quota creates resistance to the discount rule.** (Learner) A "no discount before a qualifying question" rule looks like it costs deals to a rep whose pay depends on closing. The hypothesis does not address this.
- **Reps have no picture of a good response.** (Learner) The success checks are manager-facing. Reps have no stated criteria for a good response.
- **Differentiator knowledge is a missing prerequisite.** (Learner, Alignment) "Competitor X is cheaper" and "What do we get that justifies the premium?" need product and competitor knowledge. The hypothesis assumes "product training" covers it. It does not say so.
- **The discount-rate measure may reward the wrong behavior.** (Theory) Discount rate counts avoided discounts. It does not check whether the acknowledge–pivot–qualify sequence happened. Score the sequence on observed calls instead.
- **Discount rate has no objective behind it.** (Alignment) The course teaches no discount-policy decision, so "before offering any discount" has no knowledge prerequisite.
- **No worked examples.** (Theory) The hypothesis moves straight to answering objections. For a skill, a worked example usually comes before guided and then independent practice.
- **No spaced practice.** (Theory) One 30-minute session will decay. The two live calls may fall weeks later. The course needs spaced retrieval practice.
- **Branching scope is not bounded.** (Practicality) Acknowledge, pivot, and qualify across five objections implies many branches. No branch limit is set. Rise 360 handles simple choice-based scenarios well and handles complex branching poorly. Without a cap, the build may exceed its hours or need a Storyline developer.
- **Effort does not fit the schedule.** (Practicality) A 30-minute course usually takes about 20–30 build hours. One ID must also cover SME review, WCAG 2.1 AA checks, SCORM upload and LMS testing, and a pilot. None of this is budgeted. No cut line or contingency is named.
- **Manager-side transfer is not designed.** (Theory) Transfer and retention events depend entirely on managers. The course does not include them.

### Minor issues

- The "any discount" rule is absolute. The course gives no guidance for edge cases such as pre-committed buyers or renewals.
- The source of the five objections is stated (manager interviews and CRM loss notes), so this is answered. Each objection still needs its own practice coverage.
- "Acknowledge" and "qualifying question" have no separate criteria.
- The discount-rate baseline has no stated comparison period or control group. Discount rates move with quarter and market conditions.
- Delivery context is unstated: rep schedules, time zones, LMS access, and whether 30 minutes fits quota-heavy weeks.
- The course should keep product detail as reference material, not as main content, to limit extra cognitive load for reps who already know the product.

### Theory options from the pool

These are candidates for the Theory Consideration Report in the draft stage. They are not decisions.

- **4C/ID (Complex and Problem-Based Learning):** Build the course around a whole task (one full objection call), add supportive information (why value-first works), procedural information (the 30-second structure), and part-task drills on the opener. This replaces an unstructured "answer five objections" design.
- **Self-Efficacy Theory (Bandura):** Escalate the pushback across modules so early wins come first. This addresses the confidence root cause.
- **Desirable Difficulties:** Add a time limit to responses inside the course. This is one way to approximate pressure without voice or video.
- **Spacing Effect and Retrieval Practice Effect:** Add spaced retrieval after the main session.
- **Social Learning Theory (Bandura):** Fits the manager-observation design, but no course element uses it yet.

The draft stage must name at least two rejected theories. The current brief names none.

### Summary

The behavior is clear, and the root cause is plausible. The design is not yet buildable as stated. The main gap is between what a text-only course can produce (knowledge and decision practice) and what the root cause requires (live, pressured rehearsal). The second gap is that the success measure depends on unresourced manager time.

## Goals vs. Non-Goals

* **In-Scope Goals:**
  * A 30-minute Rise 360 SCORM course on five pricing objections.
  * Teaching the three-part response: acknowledge, pivot to a differentiator, ask a qualifying question, before any discount.
  * Practice choosing and building responses inside the course, with feedback.
  * A manager observation checklist (single page, job aid) delivered with the course.
  * Live role-play (15 minutes, with the manager, after the course) as a required blended activity outside the eLearning.
  * Discount rate tracked as a 90-day lagging indicator.
* **Out-of-Scope Non-Goals:**
  * Video production and voiceover (budget and tool constraints).
  * Storyline or other non-Rise tools (constraint).
  * Full product training or competitor research (the sales director owns differentiators and scripts; see dependencies).
  * Discount policy changes (not part of this course).
  * Live pressure rehearsal inside the eLearning itself.
  * Edge cases such as renewals and pre-committed buyers, unless added in a later iteration.

## Alternatives Weighed

* **Storyline-built course with voice and timed responses.** Rejected by author: Rise 360 only.
* **Video-based scenarios.** Rejected by author: no video production budget.
* **Voiceover narration.** Rejected by author: no voiceover budget.
* **Live role-play only (no SCORM course).** Not weighed by the author. The Analysis section notes that live rehearsal is needed for the pressure element. Whether it replaces the course or supports it is an open decision.
* **Text-only scenarios with a time limit inside Rise.** Raised by the theory reviewer as a possible approximation of pressure. Not yet accepted or rejected by the author.

1. **Pressure element: narrowed.** The course builds the cognitive framework (three-part structure and five scripts).
